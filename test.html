Understanding Body-Focused Repetitive Behaviors (BFRBs)

Reviewed by the MedPractice Clinical Board · Updated March 2025 · 8 min read
Body-Focused Repetitive Behaviors (BFRBs) — including excoriation (skin-picking) disorder and trichotillomania (hair-pulling disorder) — are among the most underdiagnosed conditions seen in both dermatology and primary care settings. Recognizing and appropriately managing these disorders can significantly reduce patient distress and prevent long-term physical complications.
Overview

BFRBs are classified in the DSM-5 under Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders. Despite affecting an estimated 1–5% of the population, they are frequently misidentified as habits, self-harm, or symptoms of other conditions. Patients often present to dermatologists or primary care physicians before receiving a psychiatric or behavioral referral.

Early identification by frontline clinicians is critical. A brief screening question — "Do you find yourself repeatedly picking at your skin or pulling your hair in a way that's hard to stop?" — can open the door to diagnosis and appropriate referral.

Clinical Presentation

Excoriation disorder presents with recurrent skin lesions, often on the face, arms, or scalp. Patients may report a sense of tension before picking and relief or pleasure during the behavior. Trichotillomania presents with noticeable hair loss in irregular patterns; unlike alopecia areata, the hair loss is typically patchy and asymmetric, with broken hairs of varying lengths.
